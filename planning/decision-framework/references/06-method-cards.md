# Method Cards

Use these as compact reference cards after you already know the lane.

## First Principles

Use when the user may be trapped by convention or inherited assumptions.

Ask:

- What problem are we actually solving?
- Which constraints are real versus habitual?
- If we started fresh, would we still choose this path?

Best for:

- strategy resets
- tool or architecture dogma
- identity-level choices that feel socially preloaded

## Opportunity Cost

Use when the user is focused on what they gain but not what they give up.

Ask:

- What is the next best real alternative?
- What time, focus, money, or relationships does this consume?
- What valuable path becomes less likely if we choose this?

Best for:

- time allocation
- side projects
- hiring and prioritization

## Decision Matrix

Use when several alternatives must be compared on multiple criteria.

Best for:

- vendor selection
- hiring
- project prioritization
- location, role, or platform choice

Watch out for:

- fake precision
- hidden dealbreakers
- over-weighting the criterion that favors the preferred option

## Expected Value

Use when upside, downside, and likelihood can be estimated well enough to matter.

Ask:

- What are the major outcomes?
- How likely is each?
- What is the magnitude of each outcome?

Best for:

- investments
- insurance-like trade-offs
- bets under uncertainty

Watch out for:

- unknown tails
- emotionally unacceptable downside even when EV looks good

## Decision Tree

Use when decisions happen in stages and later information changes the value of acting now.

Ask:

- What can we choose now?
- What might we learn later?
- Which branch preserves the most useful flexibility?

Best for:

- pilot vs full rollout
- accept now vs wait
- sequence-dependent choices

## Scenario Planning

Use when the future state of the world matters more than the present comparison.

Ask:

- What are the 2-4 plausible worlds that matter?
- Which moves work across several worlds?
- What signposts suggest one world is emerging?

Best for:

- long-horizon strategy
- career decisions in unstable markets
- major investments under volatility

## OODA

Use when the environment keeps changing and no one static plan will hold.

Loop:

- observe
- orient
- decide
- act

Best for:

- negotiations
- fast-moving execution
- rollout or operating decisions

## Outside View

Use when optimism, novelty, or attachment may distort judgment.

Ask:

- What usually happens in similar cases?
- What base rate should anchor this decision?

Best for:

- startup, launch, hiring, or project forecasts

## Pre-Mortem

Use when failure would be costly and early warning signs matter.

Ask:

- Imagine this failed badly. Why?
- Which causes are within our control?
- What signals would show up first?

Best for:

- launches
- partnerships
- hiring
- major commitments

## 10/10/10 And Regret

Use when short-term emotion and long-term meaning are competing.

Ask:

- How will this feel in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years?
- Which option is more regrettable to take?
- Which is more regrettable not to take?

Best for:

- career choices
- personal transitions
- meaningful yes/no decisions
