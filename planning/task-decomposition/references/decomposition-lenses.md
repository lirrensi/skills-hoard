# Decomposition Lenses

Choose the lens that best exposes structure for the current problem.
Do not stack every lens at once.

## 1. Conceptual lens

Use when the user needs to understand a field, topic, or body of knowledge.

Break down by:

- foundations
- major domains
- core concepts
- advanced subfields

Good for:

- learning maps
- study plans
- unfamiliar technical or scientific areas

Question to ask yourself:
What would someone need to understand before the topic stops feeling like a blur?

## 2. Component lens

Use when the thing being decomposed is a system, product, machine, architecture, or organization.

Break down by:

- subsystems
- modules
- actors
- interfaces

Good for:

- software systems
- physical systems
- operating models

Question to ask yourself:
What are the major parts, and how do they fit together?

## 3. Lifecycle lens

Use when sequence, stages, or progression matter.

Break down by:

- phases
- milestones
- states
- transitions

Good for:

- user journeys
- project phases
- manufacturing or delivery pipelines

Question to ask yourself:
How does this unfold over time?

## 4. Task lens

Use when the user mainly needs execution structure.

Break down by:

- goals
- subgoals
- work packages
- deliverables

Good for:

- project planning
- implementation roadmaps
- operational checklists with hierarchy

Question to ask yourself:
What would have to be done, in what chunks, for this to become real?

## 5. Prerequisite lens

Use when the user wants to know what unlocks what.

Break down by:

- foundations before advanced topics
- gating decisions
- dependencies
- readiness conditions

Good for:

- learning order
- onboarding maps
- technical dependency planning

Question to ask yourself:
What has to come first for later branches to make sense or work?

## 6. Risk lens

Use when uncertainty, failure modes, or blind spots are central.

Break down by:

- assumptions
- blockers
- failure modes
- unknowns
- external constraints

Good for:

- strategy work
- difficult launches
- early-stage planning under uncertainty

Question to ask yourself:
What could break this, and what do we still not understand well enough?

## Choosing quickly

Use these defaults:

- field or discipline -> conceptual + prerequisite
- system or product -> component + lifecycle
- project or execution plan -> task + dependency notes
- ambiguous or risky initiative -> task or component + risk

If the first tree feels wrong, do not keep polishing it.
Switch lenses.

## Strategy overlays

If the task is complex enough, lightly overlay one of these strategies.
Do not force them into simple decompositions.

### Functional strategy

Break down by what the system or effort does.

Good for:

- workflows
- feature sets
- business processes

### Structural strategy

Break down by what the system is made of.

Good for:

- architectures
- organizations
- physical systems

### Data flow strategy

Break down by how information moves and transforms.

Good for:

- pipelines
- analytics systems
- integration-heavy processes

### Temporal strategy

Break down by sequence, stages, or timing.

Good for:

- journeys
- delivery phases
- lifecycle analysis

### Cost or resource strategy

Break down by spend, capacity, or constrained shared resources.

Good for:

- optimization work
- budgeting
- scaling bottlenecks

## Relationship types

When the tree needs extra structure, prefer these clear relationship labels:

- `prerequisite for`
- `depends on`
- `data flows into`
- `controls or triggers`
- `happens before`
- `shares resources with`
- `often confused with`
- `can run in parallel with`
