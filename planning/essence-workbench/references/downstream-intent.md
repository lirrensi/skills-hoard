# Downstream intent

Downstream intent is a preservation hint, not a presentation decision.

Use it to protect the right details during essence creation without collapsing Step 1 into delivery design.

## The rule

- Be destination-aware.
- Do not become destination-driven.

That means:

- Ask what the user thinks this may become later.
- Use that answer to decide what details must survive.
- Do not add presentation pacing, layout, narration, or file-type assumptions to the essence.

## Good intake prompts

- `Do you already know the likely final use for this?`
- `What would be painful to lose while structuring this?`
- `Will this later need to support a visual, a lesson, a discussion, or a reference doc?`

## Preservation hints by likely downstream goal

### Visual outputs

Preserve:

- labels that can appear on a page
- metrics, units, and comparison dimensions
- entities, categories, and grouping logic
- short evidence notes that justify the visual story

Do not add:

- slide order
- layout blocks
- graphic style directions

### Audio or conversational outputs

Preserve:

- questions people naturally ask
- chronology and turning points
- conflict, tension, stakes, and uncertainty
- voice-sensitive wording when it matters

Do not add:

- speaker tags
- scripted banter
- scene timing

### Instructional outputs

Preserve:

- sequence
- prerequisites
- checks, failure modes, and decision points
- examples that clarify a hard step

Do not add:

- slide beats
- lesson pacing cues
- UI interactions

### Reference or documentation outputs

Preserve:

- exact terminology
- stable hierarchy or section logic
- exceptions, caveats, and scope boundaries
- provenance and version-sensitive notes

Do not add:

- polished prose for a final document
- page layout assumptions
- publishing metadata that belongs later

## Safe heuristic

If you are unsure how much downstream intent should matter, ask:

`What detail would be the biggest disaster to lose?`

Then preserve that detail in the essence and stop there.
