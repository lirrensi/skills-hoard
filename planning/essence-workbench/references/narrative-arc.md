# Narrative arc

Narrative arc turns source material into a coherent sequence with setup, tension, and resolution. Use it when the source is best understood as a story rather than a flat list.

## Use when

- Meaning comes from change over time with stakes, reversals, and a recognizable turning point.
- The source is best understood as a story-shaped sequence instead of a neutral record.

## Do not use when

- The reader mainly needs exhaustive chronology, not a shaped arc.
- The material is better preserved as claims, metrics, or reference knowledge rather than story movement.

## Downstream intent note

- If the user already knows a likely later use, let that guide what details must survive, not how the material should be presented.
- Preserve beats, tension, and unresolved pressure, but do not add scene blocking, voiceover cues, or final storytelling format instructions here.

## Best editable shape

Prefer Markdown.

```markdown
# <story title>

## Setup
<initial state>

## Rising tension
<what changed>

## Turning point
<critical moment>

## Resolution
<outcome>

## Remaining tension
- <what is still unresolved>
```

## What good narrative content does

- Explains why events matter by linking them into a causal arc.
- Preserves stakes, tension, and meaningful change.
- Keeps the story legible without reducing it to a scene list.

## Common failure modes

- Recapping events without showing causality.
- Forcing neat resolution where the source remains unresolved.
- Flattening character, theme, or emotional movement.

## Preserve from the source

- Key beats, motivations, reversals, and turning points.
- The pacing or emphasis that shapes interpretation.
- Any remaining unresolved tension.

## Pre-save checks

- Each beat has a clear narrative job.
- The arc passes a "therefore/but" logic test, not just "and then."
- Facts and interpretation stay distinguishable.
