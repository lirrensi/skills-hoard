# Summary

A summary compresses source material into the smallest faithful version that still preserves the main point, key support, and practical stakes. Use it when the reader needs orientation fast.

## Use when

- The main job is to give someone fast orientation without losing the bottom line.
- The source has one dominant throughline that can be compressed faithfully.

## Do not use when

- The reader will need browseable questions, side-by-side options, or a reusable data structure.
- Chronology, exact wording, or argument tension matters more than compression.

## Downstream intent note

- If the user already knows a likely later use, let that guide what details must survive, not how the material should be presented.
- Keep the summary free of slide beats, narration cues, layout instructions, or file-type assumptions.

## Best editable shape

Prefer Markdown.

```markdown
---
essence: summary
confidence: medium
---

# <title>

## Core idea
<one short paragraph>

## Key points
- <point>
- <point>

## Evidence or examples
- <support>

## Open questions
- <gap>
```

## What good summary content does

- Gets to the core question, main finding, and practical stakes fast.
- Compresses without flattening the source's nuance or confidence.
- Makes the takeaway memorable, not just shorter.

## Common failure modes

- Turning a summary into a detail dump or a vague blurb.
- Removing the caveats that keep the meaning accurate.
- Repeating source language without clarifying why it matters.

## Preserve from the source

- The original emphasis, not just the raw facts.
- Key qualifiers, scope limits, and uncertainty language.
- Any term whose wording changes the conclusion.

## Pre-save checks

- A reader can tell the bottom line in one pass.
- Nothing important is overstated, softened, or omitted.
- Filler, slogans, and repeated phrasing are gone.
