# Meta-Cognition Library

Thinking about thinking — how to inspect, debug, and improve your own reasoning.

---

## Core Meta-Cognitive Practices

| Practice | What It Is | How to Apply |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Notice Confusion** | When something feels "off" — stop and investigate. Confusion is a signal, not a nuisance. | Ask: "What exactly is confusing me? What assumption is being violated?" |
| **Separate Observation from Interpretation** | What did you actually see/hear? vs What did you conclude from it? | "They didn't reply" (observation) vs "They're ignoring me" (interpretation) |
| **Hold Multiple Hypotheses** | Don't commit to one explanation. Keep 2-3 plausible hypotheses alive. | "It could be A, B, or C. What evidence would distinguish them?" |
| **What Would Change My Mind?** | Before committing to a belief, define what evidence would flip you. | If you can't name any evidence that would change your mind, you're not reasoning — you're believing. |
| **Check the Frame** | Is the problem being presented in a way that biases the answer? | "Who framed this question? What alternative framings exist?" |
| **Object-Level vs Meta-Level** | Object-level = the thing itself. Meta-level = how you're thinking about the thing. | "Am I arguing about the issue, or about how we're arguing about the issue?" |
| **Stop Rule** | When to stop analyzing and decide. Analysis has diminishing returns. | Set a deadline. Ask: "Will more thinking change my decision?" If no, stop. |

## Confidence & Calibration

| Concept | What It Is | Practice |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Confidence Calibration** | Matching your certainty to actual accuracy | Track predictions. If you say 80% and you're right 50%, you're overconfident. |
| **Degrees of Belief** | Beliefs aren't binary (true/false). They're on a spectrum. | Use percentages: "I'm 70% sure" instead of "I think so" |
| **Inside vs Outside View** | Inside = your specific case. Outside = the base rate for similar cases. | "This project feels special" (inside) vs "Most projects like this take 2x longer" (outside) |
| **Unknown Unknowns** | Things you don't even know you don't know | Ask: "What would someone with a completely different background see that I'm missing?" |

## Circle of Competence

| Zone | What It Means | How to Handle |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| **Core** | Things you know deeply — you can teach them | Trust your judgment here. Act with confidence. |
| **Edge** | Things you know somewhat — you can discuss but not teach | Seek expert input. Be cautious. Flag uncertainty. |
| **Outside** | Things you don't know — you can't even ask good questions | Defer to experts. Don't form strong opinions. Learn before deciding. |

**Key insight:** The most dangerous zone is the edge — you know enough to be confident but not enough to be right.

## Cognitive Offloading

Use external tools to free up thinking capacity:

| Tool | What It Offloads |
|------|-----------------|
| **Writing** | Working memory — get ideas out of your head |
| **Checklists** | Procedural memory — don't rely on remembering steps |
| **Diagrams** | Spatial reasoning — see relationships visually |
| **Decision journals** | Memory + hindsight bias — record your reasoning at the time |
| **Rubrics** | Evaluation criteria — standardize judgments |
| **Timers / Deadlines** | Stop rules — prevent analysis paralysis |

## Thinking Hygiene Checklist

Before making an important decision, run through this:

- [ ] **Am I tired, hungry, or emotional?** Physical state affects reasoning.
- [ ] **What's my incentive?** Am I reasoning toward truth or toward what I want?
- [ ] **What would I tell a friend?** (Outside view — we give better advice to others)
- [ ] **Am I anchoring on the first thing I heard?** Seek disconfirming information.
- [ ] **What's the base rate?** What usually happens in situations like this?
- [ ] **Am I confusing the map for the territory?** Is my model of reality accurate?
- [ ] **What would change my mind?** If nothing would, I'm not reasoning.
- [ ] **Am I optimizing for the right thing?** Goodhart's Law — am I measuring what matters?
- [ ] **Who benefits from me believing this?** Follow the incentive.
- [ ] **What am I not seeing?** What would a different person notice?

## Common Meta-Cognitive Failures

| Failure | What Goes Wrong |
|---------|----------------|
| **Bias blind spot** | Seeing biases in others but not in yourself |
| **Illusion of explanatory depth** | Thinking you understand something better than you do |
| **Curse of knowledge** | Forgetting what it's like to not know something |
| **Dunning-Kruger effect** | Low skill → high confidence. High skill → low confidence. |
| **Ego depletion** | Willpower is finite. Tired = worse decisions. |
| **Planning fallacy** | Systematically underestimating time and cost |
| **Overconfidence** | Being more certain than your accuracy warrants |
