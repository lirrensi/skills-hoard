# Social & Group Dynamics Library

How groups, networks, and collective behavior actually work. You are not a single agent — you are a node in a network.

---

## Group Behavior

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Groupthink** | Cohesive groups converge on consensus and suppress dissent. Members self-censor to maintain harmony. | Team decisions, org culture. Signs: illusion of invulnerability, collective rationalization, pressure on dissenters. Fix: assign a devil's advocate, anonymous input, leader speaks last. |
| **Social Proof** | People follow what others do, especially under uncertainty. "If everyone's doing it, it must be right." | Marketing (testimonials, "bestseller"标签), behavior change (show what大多数人 do), understanding conformity. Can be virtuous or vicious. |
| **Diffusion of Responsibility** | The more people present, the less each individual feels responsible. "Someone else will handle it." | Team design (clear ownership), crisis response (assign specific roles), accountability (make responsibilities explicit). |
| **Pluralistic Ignorance** | "No one believes this, but everyone thinks everyone else does." Everyone privately disagrees but publicly conforms. | Toxic company cultures, bad meetings, the Emperor's New Clothes. Fix: create safe channels for private dissent. |
| **Wisdom of Crowds vs Mob Stupidity** | Independent judgments aggregate well (wisdom of crowds). Correlated judgments amplify errors (mob stupidity). | Forecasting (average independent estimates), surveys (ensure independence), understanding when to trust consensus vs when it's dangerous. |
| **Narrative Capture** | Groups organized around a story become prisoners of it. The narrative becomes more important than reality. | Organizations ("we're a颠覆者"), movements, personal identity. Signs: inability to update the story despite contradictory evidence. |

## Coordination & Common Knowledge

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Common Knowledge** | Not just "everyone knows X" but "everyone knows X, and everyone knows everyone knows X." The difference matters enormously. | Coordinating teams (announcements create common knowledge), secrets (once公开, it's common knowledge), taboo-breaking, building culture. |
| **Coordination Problems** | Everyone would benefit from aligning but can't easily. No individual has incentive to move first. | Standards adoption, social norms, collective action. Solution: focal points (Schelling points), commitment devices, leaders who move first. |
| **Schelling Points** | Focal points people converge on without communication. "Where would you meet someone in NYC if you couldn't communicate?" | Design (default conventions), naming (obvious names), meeting points, game theory. People naturally converge on salient options. |
| **Stag Hunt** | Cooperation yields the best outcome, but requires trust. If you合作 and others don't, you lose everything. | Trust-building, partnerships, collective行动. Different from Prisoner's Dilemma: here, mutual cooperation is the Nash equilibrium if trust exists. |

## Incentives & Principal-Agent

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Principal-Agent Problem** | The person acting on your behalf has different interests than you do. The agent knows more than the principal. | Hiring, delegation, politics, investing. Align incentives or accept misalignment. "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." |
| **Moral Hazard** | People take more risks when they don't bear the full cost of failure. | Insurance (insured take more risks), bailouts (banks gamble with safety nets), delegation (agents take shortcuts). |
| **Incentive Design** | People do what they're rewarded for, not what you ask for. Measure what matters, or you'll get what you measure. | Management, policy, parenting. Goodhart's Law applies: once you measure it, it stops being a good measure. |
| **Cobra Effect** | Incentives go wrong — solving a problem makes it worse. Bounty on cobras → people breed cobras. | Any incentive scheme. Ask: "How could this be gamed?" before implementing. |

## Network Effects & Social Structure

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Dunbar's Number** | You can only maintain ~150 stable social relationships. Beyond that, you need structures (hierarchy, rules, culture). | Org design (teams should be <150), community management, understanding why large organizations feel impersonal. |
| **Network Effects** | Value increases as more people use it. Can be direct (phone network) or indirect (platform with more apps). | Platform strategy, standards, social systems. Winner-take-all dynamics. But also: switching costs, lock-in. |
| **Information Cascades** | People ignore their private information and follow the crowd. Once a cascade starts, it's self-reinforcing. | Markets (bubbles), technology adoption, restaurant choice. Cascades can be fragile — one公开 dissent can break them. |
| **Tribalism (Ingroup/Outgroup)** | We cooperate with "us" and defect on "them." Morality is local — we apply different standards to in-group vs out-group. | Politics, team dynamics, hiring, conflict resolution. The line between "us" and "them" is arbitrary and shift-able. |

## Status & Social Position

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Status Games** | Much human behavior is about relative position, not absolute achievement. Status is zero-sum — your gain is someone's loss. | Organizations, social media, competition, politics. Understanding why people fight over seemingly trivial things. |
| **Signaling** | Costly action to communicate hidden information. The cost makes the signal可信. | Credentials (degrees signal ability), luxury goods (wealth signal), virtue signaling (moral status), interviews (preparation signal interest). |
| **Overton Window** | The range of ideas the mainstream considers acceptable at a given time. Ideas can move from unthinkable → radical → acceptable → popular → policy. | Political strategy, advocacy, innovation framing. To make an idea acceptable, first make the more extreme version discussable. |
| **Preference Falsification** | People表达 preferences they don't actually hold to适应 social pressure. Public preferences ≠ private preferences. | Understanding revolutions (sudden preference shifts when pressure drops), polling accuracy, organizational silence. |

## How to Use This Library

These models explain why groups behave differently than individuals:

1. **When a team makes a bad decision** → Check for groupthink, pluralistic ignorance, diffusion of responsibility
2. **When coordination fails** → Check for common knowledge gaps, coordination problems, missing focal points
3. **When incentives produce perverse results** → Check for principal-agent problems, moral hazard, cobra effects
4. **When social dynamics seem irrational** → Check for status games, tribalism, preference falsification
5. **When designing systems** → Account for network effects, Dunbar's number, information cascades

**Key insight:** Most "irrational" group behavior is individually rational but collectively suboptimal. The Prisoner's Dilemma isn't a bug — it's the default state of human cooperation without trust mechanisms.
