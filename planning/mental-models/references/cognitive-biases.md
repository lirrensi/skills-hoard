# Cognitive Biases Library

~70 curated biases clustered by the task they distort. Based on the Dimara et al. (2020) classification.

---

## Estimation Biases
*Distortions when assessing quantities, probabilities, or magnitudes.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Anchoring Bias** | First piece of information disproportionately influences judgment | Seeing "$1000" then "$600" feels like a deal, even if the item is worth $400 |
| **Availability Heuristic** | What's easy to recall feels more common/probable | Overestimating plane crash risk after seeing news coverage |
| **Base Rate Neglect** | Ignoring general statistics in favor of specific information | Ignoring startup failure rates because "this one feels different" |
| **Dunning-Kruger Effect** | Unskilled overestimate ability; experts underestimate it | Beginners feel confident; experts feel uncertain |
| **Optimism Bias** | Overestimating favorable outcomes, underestimating risks | "It won't happen to me" |
| **Pessimism Bias** | Overestimating the likelihood of bad outcomes | Chronic worst-case thinking |
| **Planning Fallacy** | Systematically underestimating time, cost, and risk | "This will take 2 weeks" (it takes 8) |
| **Conservatism Bias** | Insufficiently revising beliefs when presented with new evidence | Sticking with old estimates despite new data |
| **Overconfidence Effect** | Being more certain than accuracy warrants | "95% sure" turns out to be wrong 40% of the time |
| **Gambler's Fallacy** | Believing past random events affect future probabilities | "I've flipped heads 5 times, so tails is due" |
| **Hot-Hand Fallacy** | Believing a streak of success will continue | "They're on fire, they can't miss" |
| **Insensitivity to Sample Size** | Not accounting for how small samples have high variance | Drawing conclusions from 5 data points |

## Decision Biases
*Distortions when choosing between options.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Loss Aversion** | Losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent gains feel good | Rejecting a bet with positive expected value because losing feels worse |
| **Status Quo Bias** | Preferring things to stay the same | Keeping the default insurance plan even when better options exist |
| **Sunk Cost Fallacy** | Continuing because of past investment, not future value | "We've spent too much to quit now" |
| **Framing Effect** | Drawing different conclusions from the same information depending on presentation | "90% survival rate" vs "10% mortality rate" feel different |
| **Default Effect** | Favoring the default option | Most people stick with default settings |
| **Ambiguity Effect** | Avoiding options where the probability of success is unknown | Preferring a known 50% chance over an unknown probability |
| **Decoy Effect** | Adding a dominated option changes preferences between existing options | Medium popcorn seems reasonable when large is only slightly more expensive |
| **Endowment Effect** | Valuing something more simply because you own it | Demanding more to sell something than you'd pay to buy it |
| **Action Bias** | Acting when inaction would be more effective | Changing strategy after a loss even when the strategy was sound |
| **Present Bias** | Giving stronger weight to immediate payoffs over future ones | Choosing chocolate today over fruit for next week |
| **Zero-Risk Bias** | Preferring to eliminate a small risk over reducing a larger one | Spending to remove 1% risk instead of reducing 50% risk to 25% |
| **Choice-Supportive Bias** | Remembering your choices as better than they were | "I always knew that was the right call" |

## Hypothesis Assessment Biases
*Distortions when determining if something is true or false.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Confirmation Bias** | Seeking, interpreting, and remembering information that confirms existing beliefs | Only reading news that supports your political views |
| **Belief Bias** | Evaluating argument strength based on believability of conclusion, not logic | Accepting a weak argument because you agree with the conclusion |
| **Illusory Truth Effect** | Believing statements more if they've been repeated | "I've heard it many times, so it must be true" |
| **Survivorship Bias** | Concentrating on successes, ignoring failures | "All successful founders dropped out" (ignoring those who failed) |
| **Clustering Illusion** | Seeing patterns in random data | "This stock has a pattern" (it doesn't) |
| **Illusory Correlation** | Seeing relationships between unrelated events | "Bad things happen on Fridays" |
| **Observer-Expectancy Effect** | Expecting a result and unconsciously finding it | Researcher bias in experiments |
| **Selection Bias** | Non-random sampling leading to unrepresentative conclusions | Surveying only your friends about a political issue |
| **Fluency Heuristic** | Easier-to-process information feels more true | Well-designed arguments feel more convincing regardless of content |
| **Salience Bias** | Focusing on prominent/emotional items, ignoring unremarkable ones | Worrying about terrorism (dramatic) instead of heart disease (common) |

## Causal Attribution Biases
*Distortions when explaining causes of behavior and events.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Fundamental Attribution Error** | Overemphasizing personality, underemphasizing situation when explaining others' behavior | "They're late because they're lazy" (not because of traffic) |
| **Self-Serving Bias** | Taking credit for successes, blaming failures on circumstances | "I succeeded because I'm skilled; I failed because of bad luck" |
| **Actor-Observer Bias** | Explaining own behavior situationally, others' behavior dispositionally | "I was late because of traffic; they were late because they're irresponsible" |
| **Just-World Hypothesis** | Believing people get what they deserve | "They must have done something to deserve it" |
| **Hostile Attribution Bias** | Interpreting ambiguous behavior as hostile | Assuming someone didn't reply because they're angry |
| **Illusion of Control** | Overestimating your influence over external events | Believing your ritual affects game outcomes |
| **Proportionality Bias** | Assuming big events have big causes | Accepting conspiracy theories for major events |
| **Hindsight Bias** | Seeing past events as more predictable than they were | "I knew it all along" |
| **Outcome Bias** | Judging a decision by its outcome rather than the quality of the decision at the time | Calling a risky bet "smart" because it happened to work |

## Recall Biases
*Distortions in memory and recognition.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Negativity Bias** | Better recall of unpleasant memories than pleasant ones | Remembering the one criticism among ten compliments |
| **Rosy Retrospection** | Remembering the past as better than it was | "Those were the good old days" |
| **Peak-End Rule** | Remembering experiences by their peak and end, not the average | A great vacation ruined by a bad last day |
| **Euphoric Recall** | Remembering past experiences favorably while overlooking negatives | Romanticizing a bad relationship |
| **Consistency Bias** | Remembering past attitudes as similar to current ones | "I always believed this" (you didn't) |
| **Misinformation Effect** | Memory becoming less accurate due to post-event information | Witness testimony contaminated by leading questions |
| **Google Effect** | Forgetting information that can be easily found online | Not remembering facts you know you can search |
| **Serial Position Effect** | Better recall for items at the beginning and end of a list | Remembering first and last points of a presentation |
| **Frequency Illusion (Baader-Meinhof)** | After noticing something, seeing it everywhere | Learning a new word and suddenly hearing it constantly |

## Opinion & Social Biases
*Distortions in social judgment and group dynamics.*

| Bias | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Bandwagon Effect** | Doing/believing things because many others do | "Everyone is investing in crypto" |
| **Authority Bias** | Attributing greater accuracy to authority figures | "The CEO said it, so it must be right" |
| **Halo Effect** | One positive trait spills over to overall perception | Attractive people are perceived as more competent |
| **Ingroup Bias** | Favoring members of your own group | Trusting your team's estimates over another team's |
| **False Consensus Effect** | Overestimating how much others agree with you | "Everyone thinks this way" |
| **Spotlight Effect** | Overestimating how much others notice you | Thinking everyone noticed your small mistake |
| **Social Desirability Bias** | Over-reporting socially desirable behaviors | Saying you exercise more than you do |
| **Groupthink** | Desire for harmony suppresses dissent and critical evaluation | Team agrees on bad plan because no one wants to argue |
| **Pluralistic Ignorance** | No one believes something, but everyone thinks everyone else does | Everyone in a meeting privately disagrees but no one speaks up |
| **Courtesy Bias** | Giving more socially acceptable opinions than true ones | "It's great!" when you think it's mediocre |
| **Naïve Cynicism** | Expecting more egocentric bias in others than in oneself | "They're just being selfish" (when they're not) |
| **Bias Blind Spot** | Seeing biases in others but not in yourself | "I'm objective; everyone else is biased" |

## How to Use This Library

1. **When making a decision:** Check the Decision Biases section
2. **When evaluating a claim:** Check the Hypothesis Assessment section
3. **When explaining why something happened:** Check the Causal Attribution section
4. **When estimating or predicting:** Check the Estimation Biases section
5. **When remembering or recounting:** Check the Recall Biases section
6. **When in a group or social context:** Check the Opinion & Social section

**The meta-bias:** Knowing about biases doesn't make you immune. The G.I. Joe Fallacy — "knowing is half the battle" — is itself a bias. Awareness helps, but you need structural tools (checklists, decision journals, outside views) to actually counteract them.
