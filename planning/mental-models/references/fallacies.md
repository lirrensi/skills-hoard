# Logical Fallacies Library

Errors in reasoning — patterns of invalid argument to watch for.

---

## Fallacies of Relevance

The premise is logically irrelevant to the conclusion.

| Fallacy | What It Is | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Ad Hominem** | Attacking the person, not the argument | "You're not a doctor, so your health观点 is wrong" |
| **Appeal to Authority** | X is true because expert Y said so (in domain where Y isn't expert) | "Einstein believed in God, so God exists" |
| **Appeal to Emotion** | Using feelings instead of evidence | "Think of the children!" as policy argument |
| **Red Herring** | Introducing irrelevant topic to divert | "But what about..." when cornered |
| **Straw Man** | Misrepresenting someone's argument to attack it | "So you're saying we should do nothing?" |
| **Tu Quoque** | "You do it too" — deflecting criticism by pointing to the accuser's behavior | "You smoke, so you can't lecture me on health" |
| **Appeal to Tradition** | X is good because we've always done it | "We've always done it this way" |
| **Appeal to Nature** | X is good because it's natural | "It's natural, so it must be safe" |

---

## Fallacies of Presumption

The argument assumes something that hasn't been proven.

| Fallacy | What It Is | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **False Dilemma** | Presenting only two options when more exist | "You're either with us or against us" |
| **Circular Reasoning** | Using the conclusion as a premise | "It's good because it's the best" |
| **Loaded Question** | Question contains unproven assumption | "When did you stop cheating?" |
| **Slippery Slope** | A will inevitably lead to Z without justification | "If we allow X, soon we'll have chaos" |
| **Begging the Question** | Assuming the truth of what you're trying to prove | "Ghosts exist because I've seen supernatural things" |
| **Complex Cause** | Oversimplifying a cause when multiple factors are involved | "The economy crashed because of the president" |

---

## Fallacies of Ambiguity

The argument exploits unclear meaning.

| Fallacy | What It Is | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Equivocation** | Using same word with different meanings | "The law says X" (natural law vs legal law) |
| **Composition** | Assuming what's true of parts is true of whole | "Each player is great, so the team is great" |
| **Division** | Assuming what's true of whole is true of parts | "The company is profitable, so every product is" |
| **No True Scotsman** | Redefining a group to exclude counterexamples | "No real programmer would use that tool" |

---

## Statistical & Causal Fallacies

Errors in reasoning about data, probability, and cause.

| Fallacy | What It Is | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Hasty Generalization** | Drawing conclusion from insufficient sample | "I met two rude people from X, they're all rude" |
| **Post Hoc** | Assuming causation from sequence | "I wore red and won, red brings luck" |
| **Correlation ≠ Causation** | Two things happening together doesn't mean one causes the other | Ice cream sales and drowning both rise in summer |
| **Sunk Cost** | Continuing because of past investment, not future value | "We've spent too much to quit now" |
| **Base Rate Neglect** | Ignoring general statistics in favor of specific information | Ignoring startup failure rates because "this one feels different" |
| **Regression to the Mean** | Ignoring that extreme results tend to return to average | Punishing/rewarding after an outlier performance |
| **Simpson's Paradox** | Trend appears in groups but reverses when combined | Treatment looks effective in each subgroup but harmful overall |

---

## Systemic & Narrative Fallacies

Errors that emerge from how we construct stories and systems.

| Fallacy | What It Is | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Narrative Fallacy** | We make sense of the world by weaving stories, ignoring randomness | "They succeeded because they worked hard" (ignoring luck) |
| **Hindsight Bias** | "I knew it all along" — making the past seem more predictable | After a crash: "The signs were obvious" |
| **Cobra Effect** | Incentives go wrong — solving a problem makes it worse | Bounty on cobras → people breed cobras |
| **Moral Licensing** | Doing something good gives permission to do something bad | "I recycled, so I can take this flight" |
| **Just-World Hypothesis** | The world is fair, so victims must deserve their fate | "They must have done something to deserve it" |
| **Survivorship Bias** | Focusing on successes, ignoring failures | "All billionaires dropped out of college" (ignoring those who failed) |
| **Availability Cascade** | A belief gains plausibility through repetition in public discourse | "Repeat something long enough and it becomes true" |

---

## How to Detect Fallacies

1. **Identify the conclusion** — what is the person trying to prove?
2. **Identify the premises** — what reasons are given?
3. **Check relevance** — do the premises actually support the conclusion?
4. **Check assumptions** — is something being assumed without evidence?
5. **Check definitions** — are words being used consistently?
6. **Check the data** — is the sample sufficient? Is causation claimed from correlation?
7. **Check the story** — is a compelling narrative replacing actual analysis?
