# Mental Models Library

Reusable thinking patterns — lenses for seeing situations clearly.

---

## Decision & Judgment

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Inversion** | Instead of asking how to succeed, ask how to avoid failure | Planning, risk assessment, "what could go wrong?" |
| **Second-Order Thinking** | "And then what?" — trace consequences beyond the immediate | Any decision with ripple effects |
| **Opportunity Cost** | What you give up by choosing something | Resource allocation, prioritization |
| **Expected Value** | Probability × Impact — think in distributions, not point estimates | Uncertain outcomes, gambling, startups |
| **Satisficing vs Maximizing** | "Good enough" vs "optimal" — know which game you're playing | Time-constrained decisions, perfectionism traps |
| **Regret Minimization** | Project to age 80 — which choice minimizes regret? | Big life decisions, career moves |
| **Base Rates** | What's the statistical average outcome for this class of events? | Predictions, planning, avoiding inside-view bias |
| **Reversible vs Irreversible** | Two-way doors (easy to undo) vs one-way doors (hard to undo) | Risk calibration, "should I decide fast or slow?" |
| **Kill Criteria** | Pre-defined conditions under which you stop or abandon | Projects, investments, relationships — decide exit conditions upfront |

## Systems & Complexity

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Feedback Loops** | Outputs become inputs — reinforcing (exponential) or balancing (stable) | Organizational dynamics, product growth, habits |
| **Compounding** | Small consistent gains accumulate dramatically over time | Learning, investing, relationships, habits |
| **Leverage Points** | Places where small changes produce big effects | Problem-solving, strategy, intervention design |
| **Diminishing Returns** | Each additional unit of input produces less output | Resource allocation, optimization, "when to stop" |
| **Threshold Effects** | Nothing happens until a critical point, then everything changes | Tipping points, viral growth, habit formation |
| **Network Effects** | Value increases as more people use it | Platform strategy, standards, social systems |
| **Emergence** | System behavior that can't be predicted from individual parts | Complex systems, team dynamics, markets |
| **Bottlenecks / Constraints** | The slowest step determines throughput for the whole system | Process optimization, project management (Theory of Constraints) |
| **S-Curves** | Growth starts slow, goes exponential, then plateaus | Technology adoption, skill learning, market maturity |

## Perception & Bias

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Map vs Territory** | The model is not the reality — all abstractions lose information | When relying on data, models, frameworks |
| **Survivorship Bias** | You only see the winners — the failures are invisible | Success stories, "best practices", historical analysis |
| **Availability Heuristic** | What's easy to recall feels more common/probable | Risk assessment, news consumption, fear |
| **Anchoring** | First information disproportionately influences judgment | Negotiations, estimates, pricing |
| **Confirmation Bias** | Seeking evidence that confirms what you already believe | Research, evaluation, self-reflection |

## Problem-Solving

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **First Principles** | Break down to fundamental truths, rebuild from there | Innovation, challenging assumptions, "why do we do it this way?" |
| **Occam's Razor** | Simplest explanation is usually correct | Diagnostics, debugging, hypothesis selection |
| **Chesterton's Fence** | Don't remove something until you understand why it's there | Changing systems, "improving" processes, tradition |
| **Hanlon's Razor** | Don't attribute to malice what can be explained by stupidity | Interpersonal conflicts, organizational failures |
| **Fermi Estimation** | Break unknown into estimable parts, multiply | Sanity checks, quick feasibility, "is this even plausible?" |
| **Pre-Mortem** | Assume failure, work backward to find causes | Planning, risk assessment, before committing |
| **Red Team / Blue Team** | Assign someone to attack your plan, someone to defend | Stress-testing decisions, security, strategy |

## Strategy & Action

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **OODA Loop** | Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. Faster loops beat slower loops | Fast-changing environments, competition, crises |
| **Explore vs Exploit** | Finding new options vs milking the best known option | Career choices, product development, research |
| **Positive-Sum vs Zero-Sum** | Positive-sum = both win by creating. Zero-sum = I win, you lose | Negotiations, partnerships, market analysis |
| **Comparative Advantage** | Do what you're *relatively* best at, even if you're absolutely better at everything | Delegation, team building, choosing where to compete |
| **Asymmetric Upside** | Bets where potential loss is capped but potential gain is exponential | Investing, side projects, networking |
| **Optionality** | Keep options open — value of having choices increases with uncertainty | Career planning, architecture, investments |
| **Incentives Matter** | "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome" (Munger) | Designing systems, managing teams, understanding behavior |

## Human Nature & Social Dynamics

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Mimetic Desire** | We copy what other people want — desire is imitated (Girard) | Consumer trends, status games, personal envy |
| **Status vs Wealth Games** | Wealth is positive-sum (creation); Status is zero-sum (hierarchy) | Office politics, online arguments, social dynamics |
| **Illusion of Transparency** | Overestimating how much others know your mental state | Interpersonal conflict, public speaking, design |
| **Locus of Control** | Internal (I control my fate) vs External (things happen to me) | Coaching, self-reflection, overcoming victim mentality |
| **Principal-Agent Problem** | The person acting on your behalf has different interests | Hiring, delegation, politics, investing |
| **Coordination Problems** | Everyone would benefit from aligning but can't easily | Standards, social norms, collective action |
| **Signaling** | Costly action to communicate hidden information | Credentials, luxury goods, virtue signaling |

## Creativity & Generation

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Lateral Thinking** | Solving problems through indirect, creative approaches | Brainstorming, when feeling "stuck" |
| **Divergent vs Convergent** | Divergent = create many options. Convergent = pick the best. Never both at once. | Facilitating meetings, writing, design thinking |
| **Constraint-Driven Innovation** | Artificial limits force novel pathways | Writer's block, low-budget projects, tight deadlines |
| **Morphological Box** | Break concept into attributes, mix and match randomly | Ideation, product invention |

## Growth & Skill Acquisition

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **Shu-Ha-Ri** | Follow the rule (Shu), Break the rule (Ha), Be the rule (Ri) | Learning a new skill, knowing when to improvise |
| **Zone of Proximal Development** | Sweet spot: just beyond what you can do alone, achievable with guidance | Teaching, setting goals, designing tutorials |
| **Desirable Difficulty** | Learning that requires more effort creates long-term retention | Studying, practicing, reading dense material |
| **Deliberate Practice** | Targeted effort at the edge of ability, with feedback | Skill development, coaching |
| **Transfer Learning** | Applying learning from one domain to another | Generalists, cross-functional roles, creativity |
| **Beginner's Mind** | Expertise creates blindness to obvious questions. Approach with fresh eyes. | Innovation, fresh perspectives, avoiding curse of knowledge. Ask "why do we do it this way?" |

## Practical Action Models

| Model | What It Is | When to Use |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** | Build the smallest thing that delivers value to test a hypothesis. Not a crappy version — the最小 version that tests核心 assumptions. | Launching a project, learning a skill, testing an idea. Don't build the whole thing — build enough to learn. |
| **Parkinson's Law** | Work expands to fill the time available. Give yourself a week, it takes a week. Give yourself a day, it takes a day. | Procrastination, setting deadlines, resource constraints. Use artificial deadlines to force completion. |
| **Bounded Rationality** | We can't be perfectly rational — we have limited time, information, and cognitive capacity. We "satisfice" (pick the first good enough option) rather than optimize. | Accepting that "perfect" is the enemy of "done." Set a "good enough" threshold and stop searching when you find it. |
| **MECE Principle** | Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive. Break things down so nothing overlaps and nothing is left out. | Structuring arguments, problem decomposition, building dashboards, organizing information. If it's not MECE, you have gaps or double-counting. |
| **OODA Loop** | Observe → Orient → Decide → Act. Faster loops beat slower loops. The fighter pilot decision cycle. | Fast-changing environments, competition, crises. Speed of iteration matters more than perfection of any single iteration. |
| **Two-Way vs One-Way Doors** | Two-way doors = reversible decisions (decide fast). One-way doors = irreversible decisions (decide slow). | Risk calibration. Most decisions are two-way doors — decide quickly and correct. Reserve deliberation for true one-way doors. |

## Philosophical Razors

| Razor | What It Is |
|-------|-----------|
| **Hitchens's Razor** | What can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence |
| **Sagan Standard** | Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence |
| **Brandolini's Law** | The energy to refute bullshit is an order of magnitude larger than to produce it |
| **Cromwell's Rule** | Never assign 0% or 100% probability to anything |
| **Gall's Law** | All complex working systems evolved from simple working systems |
| **Goodhart's Law** | When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure |
