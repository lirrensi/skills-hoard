# LinkedIn Best Practices

## Platform Characteristics

- **Character limit**: 3,000 characters for posts, 125,000 for articles
- **Best for**: Thought leadership, professional insights, B2B
- **Audience**: Professionals, decision-makers, recruiters
- **Tone**: Professional but human, expertise-focused

## Post Types

### Short Feed Post
Best for: Quick insights, updates, reactions, questions

**Structure:**
1. Hook (first 1-2 lines, visible before "see more")
2. Body (story, insight, or value)
3. CTA (question or engagement prompt)

**Length:** 1,200-1,500 characters optimal

### Long-Form Article
Best for: Deep dives, comprehensive guides, evergreen content

**Structure:**
- Formal article with H2 headings
- Introduction with hook
- Body with sections
- Conclusion with CTA

**Use when:**
- Content is evergreen and reference-worthy
- Need structured argument with headings
- Building newsletter subscriber base

### Carousel
Best for: Frameworks, step-by-step guides, lists, comparisons

**Structure:**
- Slide 1: Hook/Title
- Slides 2-9: One point per slide
- Slide 10: Summary + CTA

**Tips:**
- Document format (PDF) works best
- Each slide should be readable alone
- Design for vertical scrolling

## Best Practices

### The "See More" Problem
LinkedIn truncates after ~2 lines. Your hook must:
- Be in the first 1-2 lines
- Stand alone as compelling
- Not be a generic "I'm excited to share..."

### Hook Formulas That Work
- "[Unexpected outcome] — here's the story:"
- "[Contrarian take] Here's why:"
- "[Number] things I learned about [topic]:"
- "Most people think [X]. The truth is [Y]."
- Story hook: "I almost [mistake]..."

### What Works
- Personal stories with business lessons
- Contrarian takes on industry topics
- Behind-the-scenes of building something
- Data and original insights
- Polls that spark discussion (but don't build authority)

### What Doesn't Work
- Links in post body (kills reach — put in comments)
- Overly promotional content
- Generic motivational quotes
- Corporate speak without personality
- Tagging people who don't know you

### Content That Performs Well
- Lessons learned from failures
- Original frameworks or mental models
- Behind-the-scenes of decision-making
- Industry predictions with reasoning
- Specific metrics and results

### Engagement Tips
- First hour engagement matters most
- Comments > reactions > clicks
- Respond to all comments
- Ask experience-based questions (not generic "agree?")

## Format Guidelines

### Line Breaks
- Use frequent line breaks (empty line between paragraphs)
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences max)
- Lists with emojis or symbols for scanning

### Hashtags
- 3-5 relevant hashtags maximum
- Place at end or naturally in text
- Use industry-specific, not generic

### Mentions
- Tag people only if relevant and they know you
- Thank people who inspired content

### Links
- Put links in FIRST COMMENT, not post body
- Or use "Link in comments" approach
