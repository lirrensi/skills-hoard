# Framing And Prompting

Good brainstorming starts with a usable brief.

If the frame is weak, ideation becomes high-output irrelevance.

## Capture The Brief

Ask or infer:

- what problem or opportunity this is really about
- what artifact is needed at the end: raw list, clusters, shortlist, ranked picks, or next experiments
- who the ideas are for or who they affect
- what constraints are real versus assumed
- what has already been tried, rejected, or overused
- what makes an idea good in this context
- how much spread the user wants: safe, balanced, or wild

## Minimal Brief Template

Use this when the user gives you very little:

```text
Topic:
Real problem:
Who this is for:
Desired output:
Real constraints:
Assumed constraints to challenge:
What good looks like:
What has already been tried:
```

## Frame Tightening Moves

Use these when the brief is fuzzy:

- convert a vague topic into a "How might we..." question
- separate the symptom from the real job to be done
- split one giant prompt into smaller sub-problems if needed
- ask whether the user wants novelty, speed, feasibility, legitimacy, or learning most
- make non-goals explicit so the idea set does not drift

Examples:

- `How might we reduce onboarding churn without adding headcount?`
- `How might we create three naming directions for a serious B2B product?`
- `How might we expand our architecture options before deciding on a rewrite?`

## Constraint Handling

Always separate:

- `real constraints`: budget cap, legal rules, timeline, hard technical limits
- `assumed constraints`: habit, internal politics, legacy preference, imagined user expectations

Good brainstorms respect the first set and pressure-test the second.

## Variety Targets

Decide what kind of spread is needed:

- `practical spread`: several realistic options with moderate variation
- `balanced spread`: practical, ambitious, and unconventional options together
- `wild spread`: push far outside the local optimum before narrowing later

If the user does not specify, use `balanced spread`.

## Prompt Ladder

Move down this ladder when the session starts narrowing too early:

1. `What are the obvious ways to do this?`
2. `What are three materially different mechanisms?`
3. `What would the opposite approach look like?`
4. `What changes if time, budget, or scale shifts by 10x?`
5. `What would another field do here?`
6. `What is the weird idea worth keeping alive for five more minutes?`

## Stop Signs

Do not start heavy ideation yet if:

- the user is mixing two or more unrelated problems
- success criteria are completely missing
- the output shape is unclear and that changes the work materially
- the user really wants a decision, not more options

Fix the frame first.
