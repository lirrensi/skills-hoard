# Human Sentences: Verbs, Agency, and Rhythm

> Stable craft reference. Sentence moves that replace machine smoothness with filmed action and spoken cadence. Changes only when the craft understanding changes, not when new slop patterns appear.

### 1. Carrier-Verb Collapse

**Move:** Find the verb hiding inside the abstract noun and let it do the work.

**Do:** Hunt provide, carry out, conduct, ensure, perform, implement, achieve plus an abstract noun (provision, implementation, training, analysis). Delete the carrier verb and conjugate the noun: provision becomes provide, training becomes train. One actor plus one strong verb per clause.

Before: "Our work is providing security and conducting training for new hires."

After: "We guard factories. We train new hires."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Отглагольное существительное» (chunk_03); inventory D13

### 2. Actor Revival

**Move:** Put a visible actor back into every action, four ways.

**Do:** (a) Name the actor: flip passive to person or firm plus active verb. Genuine states with no actor get the shortest state phrase ("the house is ready"). (b) Subjects must act: cleaning, implementation, and growth cannot do anything, so rebuild the sentence around people, firms, or machines. (c) State verbs become visible: indicates or ensures becomes an action you can film plus a number. (d) Uncompress abstractions: "rising competition and slowing growth" becomes who acts, what they want, and where the money goes.

Before: "Reforestation brought down CO2 concentration and implementation of the program ensured efficiency."

After: "Volunteers replanted that stretch. CO2 levels dropped. The crew now finishes each block in two days."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Страдательный залог», «Больше действий» (chunk_03/04); inventory D14; merges backlog #12

### 3. Officialese to Plain Word

**Move:** Trade the lawyer-proof noun for the one reader-facing verb plus the deciding detail.

**Do:** Replace "issuance is carried out" with where to pick it up. Replace ceremony ("kindly", "sorry to bother", "thanks in advance") with logistics: attach the document, give the deadline, explain why, open a question channel. Cut any formal detail that changes no decision.

Before: "Funds are credited no later than two days via the partner network. Kindly await notification. Thanks in advance for your understanding."

After: "We credit the money within two days. Top up at Svyaznoy, Kiwi, or Euroset. MKB cards do not work. Questions: write to support, we answer same day."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Канцеляризмы и формализм», «Видимость вежливости» (chunk_02:477, chunk_03); inventory D15

### 4. Unpack Indirect Speech

**Move:** Strip report-speak scaffolding down to the claim itself.

**Do:** Delete "states that", "believes that", "expects that", "is of the opinion that" unless the hedging or the attribution carries meaning (see entry 10). What remains is a direct verb or noun phrase.

Before: "I believe that quitting is the least useful way to fix the text."

After: "Quitting is the least useful way to fix the text."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Косвенная речь, воля и познание» (chunk_05:119-173); inventory D16

### 5. Read-Aloud Stumble Gate

**Move:** Use your voice as the syntax linter. Every sentence must survive speaking speed.

**Do:** Read the draft aloud at normal speaking pace. Anywhere you stumble, lose the thread, run out of breath, or stack commas, split the sentence, simplify the clause order, or delete the aside sitting between paired commas or parentheses. No sentence ships that your lungs cannot finish.

Before: "After delivery, if you gave in to the client and sent the file by mail, an internal review starts, which can last for weeks, if you do not control it, which is dangerous."

After: "Send the file by mail without agreeing on review terms and the client goes quiet for weeks. Set the review date before you send. Otherwise you lose control of the process."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Проверка на глаз и на слух», «Исправляйте всё, что трудно читать» (chunk_02:509, chunk_05:19); inventory E17

### 6. Splice Split and Subordination Cap

**Move:** Break glued sentences at the joints and cap clause depth at two.

**Do:** For conjunction-free compounds, test each half standalone. If both survive, use a period. Map clause hierarchy; at depth three or more, split and promote the middle step to its own sentence.

Before: "Because of a law adopted despite industry protests, all companies that transmit data must keep message history for a year."

After: "A new law forces data carriers to keep message history for a year. It passed despite industry protests."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Разделяйте слипшиеся предложения», «Лишнее подчинение» (chunk_05:21-117); inventory E18

### 7. Run Collapse and Parcelation Carve-Out

**Move:** Shrink synonym runs to the sharpest member. Save fragments for the peak only.

**Do:** For near-synonym runs, keep the most precise member and cut the rest. For runs with distinct info, develop one member with an example instead of stacking adjectives. Fragment sentences (parcelation) are allowed only at the emotional or logical peak, buried mid-paragraph, then return to full sentences. Never use fragments as a default rhythm spice.

Before: "After two months of long and tedious editing we reached a normal, acceptable version. The text was short, laconic, dry, unemotional, restrained, but also dull."

After: "After two months of editing we had a short version. It was dull. Correct, tidy, and dead. We had wasted the time."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Однородные члены», «Парцелляция» (chunk_05:329-445); inventory E19

### 8. Delete Entities Before Explaining Terms

**Move:** Ask whether the reader needs the term at all before defining it.

**Do:** When a term is defined only by other unknown terms, stop. If the reader does not need the entity, delete the sentence. If they do, define each link from what the reader already knows, building from the familiar outward.

Before: "GPIO supports PWM and ADC on pins five through nine."

After: "The chip has simple pins. Tell pin five to send current and a light turns on. That is all you need for the first circuit."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «От простого к сложному» (chunk_06:837-869); inventory E20

### 9. Punctuation Repertoire: Colon, Parens, Period

**Move:** Replace the banned dash with three tools that each do one job.

**Do:** Use the colon for setup (announce a list, quote, or payoff). Use parentheses for asides (a whisper that interrupts once, then closes). Use the period for punch (cut the clause free and let it land). If a sentence wants a dash, pick which of the three jobs it is doing and use that mark.

Before: "There is one rule for endings, never blur them."

After: "There is one rule for endings: never blur them. (Blurred endings read as evasion.) Never blur them."

**Source:** Decision D2; v2 §11

### 10. Calibrated Confidence

**Move:** Hedge only where the evidence is thin, and always attach the reason.

**Do:** Keep true hedges (probably, might, I do not know) with the evidence gap named next to them. Match certainty to evidence: strong data earns flat assertion. Use "I think" only against a named opposing view. Delete performative hedges ("honestly", "to be frank", "frankly") everywhere.

Before: "Honestly, the new policy will improve retention. It will reduce churn."

After: "The new policy might improve retention: early numbers cover two teams and six weeks. Whether it reduces churn, I have no idea yet."

**Source:** Decision D5; v2 Certainty (497-510)

### 11. Meaning-Driven Rhythm

**Move:** Let the difficulty of the thought set the pace, not a word-count formula.

**Do:** Slow down at the hard part (longer sentence, full clause, room to follow). Cut to short sentences at the punchline. Fragments only at peaks (see entry 7). Never apply fixed length recipes.

Before: "The deploy failed. It was bad. We fixed it fast."

After: "The deploy failed because the migration locked the users table while the retry queue kept hammering it. We rolled back. Then we banned Friday deploys."

**Source:** Decision D6; inventory E17-E19

### 12. Rhythm Without the Recipe

**Move:** Break metronomic cadence by ear, never by counting words.

**Do:** Vary sentence length and shape until no two adjacent paragraphs share the same beat. Allowed tools: fragments at peaks, a trailing ellipsis once per text at most, a one-sentence paragraph for the line that matters. The old fixed recipe (fixed long/short word counts) is dead and must never be repeated.

Before: "The project started in March. The team worked hard. They shipped in June. Users liked it."

After: "The project started in March, which feels like forever ago. The team worked hard. Really hard. They shipped in June, and users actually liked it."

**Source:** v2 Rhythm (467-481), D6-reworked

### 13. Certainty With Reasons

**Move:** Vary confidence the way a person with uneven evidence does.

**Do:** Mix flat assertion (where you know), qualified guess (where evidence is partial, with the gap stated), and open ignorance (where you lack data). Never stack uniform "will" claims.

Before: "The new policy will improve retention. It will reduce churn. It will increase satisfaction."

After: "The new policy might improve retention. It probably reduces churn. Whether it increases satisfaction? I have no idea."

**Source:** v2 Certainty (497-510), D5-reworked

### 14. Register Mix

**Move:** Let one plain phrase into the formal draft, then return to neutral.

**Do:** Use contractions in some sentences, not all. Drop a single casual phrase or idiom into formal text, then revert. The contrast is the human signal; sustained slang is a costume.

Before: "The implementation demonstrates a robust approach to error handling."

After: "The error handling is solid, which is good, because the previous version fell over if you looked at it wrong."

**Source:** v2 Register (525-538)

### 15. Imperfect Connection

**Move:** Connect sentences the way speech does: sometimes cleanly, sometimes barely.

**Do:** Start an occasional sentence with And, But, So, or Because. Delete transition words where the logic is obvious. Let one paragraph barely connect to the next. Add at most one flow-interrupting aside per section.

Before: "Speed matters. Therefore, caching is important. Additionally, it reduces costs."

After: "Speed matters. Caching helps. It also costs less, if you do it right."

**Source:** v2 Connection (539-553)

### 16. Self-Correction and Asides

**Move:** Correct yourself on the page once in a while, the way thinking aloud works.

**Do:** Insert a parenthetical that questions or clarifies. Say "actually" and change direction. Admit a small confessional limit. One per section at most; more reads as shtick.

Before: "The meeting was productive and aligned the team."

After: "The meeting was productive. Or at least it felt productive, which might be the same thing at 9 a.m."

**Source:** v2 Self-correction (569-583)

### 17. Repetition and Parallelism Breaks

**Move:** Break one parallel structure on purpose so the text stops scanning as generated.

**Do:** Repeat a word deliberately for pressure. Let one list item take a different grammatical form. Break parallel structure once per list or triplet, not everywhere.

Before: "The app is fast. The app is reliable. The app is secure."

After: "The app is fast. Reliable too. Security is the part they keep patching."

**Source:** v2 Repetition (584-597)
