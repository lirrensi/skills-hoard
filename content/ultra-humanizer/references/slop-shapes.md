### 1. Significance Inflation

**Flags:** stands as, serves as, testament to, pivotal moment, broader trend, underscores the importance, symbolizes, sets the stage for, indelible mark, evolving landscape, highlights the importance, demonstrates the power of.

**Fix:** Cut the ceremony. State the dated fact and stop.

**Before:** "The studio was founded in 2010, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of indie game development."

**After:** "The studio was founded in 2010."

**Before:** "The failed launch highlights the importance of stakeholder alignment."

**After:** "The launch failed because nobody checked whether the data matched production."

**Before:** "The bakery's opening marks a significant moment in the neighborhood's ongoing evolution."

**After:** "A bakery opened on 5th Street in March."

**Source:** v2 section 2

### 2. Superficial -ing Analyses and Metaphor Overdrive

**Flags:** highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing, contributing to, showcasing, fostering, encompassing. Metaphors: symphony, orchestration, dance, choreography, tapestry, mosaic, beacon, masterclass, alchemy, catalyst.

**Fix:** Split the sentence. Name the agent. Replace the metaphor with the literal process.

**Before:** "The color palette uses blue and green, reflecting the region's deep connection to the sea."

**After:** "The color palette uses blue and green. The designer said she wanted it to feel coastal."

**Before:** "The frontend and backend perform a delicate dance, orchestrating a symphony of data retrieval."

**After:** "The frontend requests data from the API and renders it."

**Source:** v2 section 5

### 3. Vague Attribution and the Consensus Trap

**Flags:** experts argue, industry reports, observers have noted, some critics say, many believe, studies show, widely known, organizations across industries, many companies, leading brands, a lot of. Elastic plurals: various, a range of, in many cases, more and more citizens.

**Fix:** Name the source or delete the claim. Replace vague plurals with counts, names, dates. Consensus trap rule: known fact gets cut; unknown presented as known gets a named claimant. Elastic plural rule: every vague plural gets a count or names, or it is cut.

**Before:** "Experts believe the river plays a crucial role in the regional ecosystem."

**After:** "A 2019 survey by the Chinese Academy of Sciences found three endemic fish species in the river."

**Before:** "Organizations across industries are adapting to changing market dynamics."

**After:** "Three companies we talked to changed their pricing last quarter."

**Before:** "More and more citizens prefer domestic goods."

**After:** "April 2016: 19 percent preferred domestic nonfood goods; 40 percent chose imports (FOM poll)." (Or cut if no poll exists.)

**Before:** "It is no secret that smoking makes men handsomer."

**After:** "Cigarette makers assure us smoking makes men handsomer." (Known fact: cut. Claimed fact: name who claims it.)

For the full climb from sweeping claim to sourced fact or honest admission, see human-reader.md (truth ladder).

**Source:** v2 section 6; backlog elastic plural pattern; inventory items 3, 12, 2

### 4. Contrast Gimmicks and Binary Structures

**Flags:** not just X it is Y; it is not X it is Y; no X no Y just Z; not merely X but Y; less X more Y; where others X we Y; whether you are a seasoned X or a curious Y; both X and Y; not only X but also Y.

**Fix:** State the actual point directly. Keep contrast only when the denied half is a real belief the reader holds.

**Before:** "It is not just a tool; it is a transformation in how teams collaborate."

**After:** "It changes how teams collaborate."

**Before:** "Whether you are a seasoned system administrator or a curious hobbyist just starting your journey, Linux has something to offer."

**After:** "Linux is difficult to learn, but it gives you total control over your hardware."

**Before:** "Not only does the app allow you to save files, but it also lets you rename them."

**After:** "You can save and rename files in the app."

**Source:** v2 section 7 (covers backlog #14, #15, #20, #27 verify pass: no separate entries needed)

### 5. Tricolon and Mechanical Symmetry

**Flags:** adjective, adjective, and adjective with alliteration; lists forced into triplets; innovation, inspiration, and industry insights; three fragment bursts like Speed. Quality. Cost.

**Fix:** Keep the item that carries the claim, drop rank fillers. Two items allowed. Uneven lists allowed. Develop distinct items with examples instead of compressing to triplets.

**Before:** "fast, flexible, and future-proof"

**After:** "fast and flexible"

**Before:** "The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities."

**After:** "The event has talks, panels, and time to talk between sessions."

**Source:** v2 section 8; Rule of Three example addition folded; inventory item 19 corollary

### 6. Formulaic Structures and Closure Addiction

**Flags:** Despite X Y faces several challenges; Despite these challenges Z continues to thrive; Challenges and Future Prospects; Challenges and Legacy; In conclusion; The future looks bright; Exciting times lie ahead; full overview shape (intro to context to benefits to challenges to future to conclusion). Forced lesson at the end.

**Fix:** Cut the boilerplate. Genre fork: essay and blog may end unresolved, on a question, or provisionally. Commercial, about, and request texts end on a concrete next act plus its constraint plus a deadline (craft detail: see human-care.md).

**Before:** "Despite these challenges, the company remains committed to excellence as it continues its journey toward innovation."

**After:** "The company plans to open two more locations next year."

**Before:** "While challenges remain, the experience ultimately demonstrates the power of resilience."

**After (essay):** "The project shipped. Some people still think it was a mistake."

**After (commercial):** "Call before Thursday and we bake it for Friday. Today is the one turnaround we cannot do."

**Source:** v2 section 9; Formulaic example addition folded; inventory item 27 and decision D4

### 7. Structural Tells and Passive Leads

**Flags:** emojis in headings or bullets; boldface header lists; title case headings; curly quotes in technical prose; inline header vertical lists; fragmented headers (heading plus one sentence restatement); three fragment bursts; numbered forced outlines; identical paragraph lengths; every paragraph ending with a takeaway. Leads: Equipped with X, armed with these insights, backed by Y, empowered by. Transitions: But how does this work in practice? So why does this matter? What does this mean for X? How can we achieve this?

**Fix:** Convert to plain prose, sentence case headings, straight quotes. Use direct transitions that state the next content.

**Before:**

> ## Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships
>
> - **Speed:** Code generation is significantly faster.
> - **Quality:** Output quality has been enhanced.
> - **Adoption:** Usage continues to grow.

**After:**

> ## Strategic negotiations and global partnerships
>
> Code generation is faster now. Training improvements lifted output quality. Usage is still growing.

**Before:** "Armed with this data, marketing teams can craft highly targeted campaigns."

**After:** "Marketing teams use this data to target their ads."

**Before:** "Automating your tests saves time. But how do we actually implement this without breaking our existing pipeline?"

**After:** "To automate your tests without breaking the pipeline, start with a staging environment."

Module exception: TLDR, short summary, disclaimer, and reader briefing openers at section starts are allowed as commercial and literary devices. They orient the reader. Craft detail: see human-structure.md.

**Source:** v2 section 13; Structural example addition folded; inventory item 23 and decision D3 (covers backlog #19, #23, #26 verify pass: no separate entries needed)

### 8. Spatial Copulas and False Agency

**Flags:** stands as, serves as, lives in, boasts, emerges, becomes a fix, acts as, stands as a beacon, acts as a bridge.

**Fix:** Use simple is, are, or has. Or name the human actor.

**Before:** "The tool serves as a catalyst for innovation."

**After:** "The tool helps teams build faster."

**Before:** "The library stands as a beacon of knowledge in the community."

**After:** "The library has the best local history collection in the neighborhood."

**Before:** "The program acts as a bridge between communities."

**After:** "The program enrolls teenagers from both neighborhoods in one workshop."

**Source:** v2 section 15; Spatial Copulas example addition folded

### 9. The Five Structural Separators

**Flags:** five shape checks that separate strong explanatory prose from slop on matched pairs: (1) slow arrival, (2) abstract subjects, (3) claims without evidence, (4) failure plus commitment endings, (5) narrow mid length band.

**Fix:** (1) Move the claim to sentence one and delete warmup. (2) Replace abstract subjects with actor or number. (3) Attach source to claim or cut the claim; never invent one. (4) Cut boilerplate endings; end on fact, next step, or tension. (5) Vary length for meaning: slow at the hard part, cut at the punchline, fragments only at peaks.

**Before:** "It is important to note that the spectrum of business activity refers to different stages at which value is created."

**After:** "Businesses create value in three stages."

**Before:** "The significance of the village is evident in its continued importance as a cultural center."

**After:** "People still gather in the village for the spring festival."

**Before:** "Engagement dropped significantly, according to several sources."

**After:** "Engagement dropped 30 percent last quarter, per the internal dashboard." (Or cut if no source exists.)

**Before:** "Despite these challenges, the town continues to thrive as an integral part of regional growth."

**After:** "Traffic got worse after three IT parks opened in 2015."

**Before:** "The project started in March. The team worked hard. They shipped in June."

**After:** "The project started in March, which feels like forever ago. The team worked hard. Really hard. They shipped in June after a 30 hour final week nobody wants to repeat."

**Source:** v2 section 18; fixed length recipe retired per decision D6

### 10. Logical Stupidity: Pattern Free but Still Empty

**Flags:** clean phrases, empty paragraph. Test: delete the sentence. If no information drops, cut it. Each paragraph must advance a claim, add evidence, or give a mechanism.

**Fix:** Cut, not reword. Sub patterns: tautology loop; list as content; repetition as substance; inference from nothing; self sourced promo; significance laundering; meta recursion loop; semantic loop dialogue; resilience porn; sycophancy plus generic recipe.

**Before:** "Rainforests consist of tropical evergreen forests on both Peninsular and East Malaysia. Malaysian rainforests cover large areas of both Peninsular and East Malaysia."

**After:** "Malaysia has tropical evergreen forest on both Peninsular Malaysia and Borneo."

**Before:** "No detailed standalone description is available, but general characteristics can be inferred from its genus. The original 1912 description would have the precise details. Shell length is about 30 mm."

**After:** "No detailed description in available sources. Shell length is about 30 mm."

**Before:** "The manufacturer promoted it for memory and learning. The company cited a company sponsored study."

**After:** "The manufacturer claims it supports memory, citing its own study."

**Before:** "Thanks for reaching out! Happy to help! Hand gestures sound interesting and innovative! However this is complex, involving vision, ML, sensors. General steps: 1. Research existing solutions like GestureTek."

**After:** "Webcam gesture control lives or dies on lighting and latency. Decide that first, then pick the sensor."

Answer first: open with the reader task, answer, or hardest constraint. No chatbot framing, no roadmap sentence. (Full move: see human-reader.md.)

**Source:** v2 section 19

### 11. The From X to Y False Spectrum

**Flags:** everything from simple X to complex Y; endpoints listed to sound comprehensive while naming nothing checkable.

**Fix:** Name the two concrete uses. Cut the spectrum frame.

**Before:** "The tool handles everything from simple data entry to complex machine learning algorithms."

**After:** "You can use it to log spreadsheets or train models."

**Before:** "Readers range from beginners to experts."

**After:** "New hires use the checklist. Senior staff skip to the API table."

**Source:** backlog #17
