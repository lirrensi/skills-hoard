### 1. AI Vocabulary and Buzzword Clusters

**Flags:** delve, tapestry, landscape, interplay, nuanced, multifaceted, robust, holistic, pivotal, crucial, vital, foster, facilitate, leverage, navigate, underscore, highlight, embark, beacon, realm, intricate, meticulous, showcase, exemplify, garner, unpack, surface (verb), streamline, optimize, utilize, deploy, empower, enhance, ensure, actionable, impactful, strategic, scalable, seamless, intuitive, user-friendly, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-generation, orbit, ladder, gradient, vector, orthogonal (all used metaphorically).

**Fix:** Replace with plain words. "Delve into" becomes "look at". "Leverage" becomes "use". "Pivotal" becomes "key" or gets deleted.

**Before:** "We must leverage our robust infrastructure to navigate this complex landscape."

**After:** "We need to use the infrastructure we already have."

**Before:** "Let's unpack the key drivers behind this shift."

**After:** "Here is what changed."

**Source:** v2 §1, backlog vocab adds.

### 2. Formal Transition Stack

**Flags:** furthermore, moreover, additionally, consequently, subsequently, indeed (as sentence openers stacking formal logic).

**Fix:** Use "also", "and", or just start the sentence.

**Before:** "Furthermore, the system offers automatic backups. Moreover, it encrypts them."

**After:** "The system also backs up your files automatically and encrypts them."

**Source:** v2 §1.

### 3. Fast-Paced Openers

**Flags:** in today's fast-paced world, in an ever-changing landscape, in a rapidly evolving market, in this modern era, in today's fast-paced digital landscape.

**Fix:** Delete the opener. Start with the concrete claim.

**Before:** "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, businesses must adapt quickly to survive."

**After:** "If businesses don't update their software, they lose customers to faster competitors."

**Source:** v2 §1.

### 4. Intensifiers Without Numbers

**Flags:** significantly, dramatically, substantially, exponentially, maximally, absolutely, incredibly, truly, very, highly, genuinely, guaranteed (with no metric attached).

**Fix:** Give the number or delete the intensifier. An amplifier stacked on an evaluation steals attention from the main word and signals the author is trying too hard to convince. Readers trust facts; let them conclude "very" on their own.

**Before:** "The change dramatically improved performance."

**After:** "Latency dropped from 200 ms to 40 ms."

**Before:** "We offer maximally выгодные (favorable) rates: unlimited internet, films, and games for 350 a month."

**After:** "Unlimited internet, films, and games for 350 a month."

**Source:** backlog #9; Ilyakhov «Не усиливайте оценку» (pairing rule: amplifier plus evaluation).

### 5. The "Real" Subclass (True Professionals)

**Flags:** real professionals, true experts, true quality, genuine masters, authentic craftsmanship (the word "real" used to sound convincing).

**Fix:** Calling someone "real" never makes them more real. Replace with a fact or a short story: years of practice, hiring bar, process detail.

**Before:** "Our clinic employs true professionals."

**After:** "Our clinic hires practicing doctors with at least 7 years of medical experience."

**Source:** Ilyakhov «Настоящий».

### 6. Not-All-X Hedging

**Flags:** not all X are Y, but...; not every X...; while some X... (hedging a general claim instead of making the specific one).

**Fix:** Make the specific claim directly.

**Before:** "Not all startups fail, but many struggle with product-market fit."

**After:** "Startups often struggle with product-market fit."

**Source:** backlog #13.

### 7. Promotional Hype Words

**Flags:** breathtaking, stunning, vibrant, nestled in the heart of, rich cultural heritage, natural beauty, must-visit, groundbreaking, renowned, picturesque, world-class, best-in-class, premium, exclusive, tailored, seamless experience, frictionless, hassle-free, peace of mind.

**Fix:** Replace with one specific detail or a plain verb.

**Before:** "Nestled in the heart of the breathtaking Alps, the vibrant town boasts a rich cultural heritage."

**After:** "The town sits in a valley below the Alps. The local museum has a 16th-century woodcut collection."

**Source:** v2 §3.

### 8. Press-Release Emotion

**Flags:** We are thrilled/delighted/excited/proud to announce, empowers your business.

**Fix:** State the news. The emotion is implied or irrelevant.

**Before:** "We are thrilled to announce the launch of our new feature."

**After:** "We launched the new feature today."

**Source:** v2 §3, backlog #6 (duplicate confirmed, no new sentences).

### 9. Dead Metaphors and Corporate Cliches

**Flags:** double-edged sword, tip of the iceberg, perfect storm, low-hanging fruit, move the needle, circle back, at the end of the day, drive solutions, build alignment, facilitate synergy, the bottom line is.

**Fix:** Replace with the literal claim.

**Before:** "The new policy is a double-edged sword."

**After:** "The policy helps retention but hurts hiring speed."

**Before:** "The bottom line is that we need to move faster."

**After:** "We need to ship by Friday."

**Source:** v2 §3, backlog #11 (only "the bottom line is" was missing from v2; rest duplicates).

### 10. Historical Framing Cliches

**Flags:** gone are the days, in the age of, with the advent of.

**Fix:** Say the actual change.

**Before:** "In the age of remote work, office culture has changed forever."

**After:** "Remote work killed the daily commute for a lot of people."

**Source:** backlog #10.

### 11. The Adjective Stack

**Flags:** seamless and intuitive, robust and scalable, dynamic and engaging, thoughtful and nuanced, vibrant and diverse, clear and concise.

**Fix:** Use one adjective or a specific noun.

**Before:** "We provide a seamless and intuitive user experience."

**After:** "The app is easy to use."

**Source:** v2 §4, backlog #16 (duplicate confirmed).

### 12. Double-Noun Compound Verbs

**Flags:** to solve becomes to drive solutions; to use becomes to leverage capabilities; to talk becomes to build alignment; to change becomes to undergo a transformation; to drive operational efficiency.

**Fix:** Use the simple verb.

**Before:** "We help companies undergo digital transformations to drive operational efficiency."

**After:** "We help companies upgrade their software so they can work faster."

**Source:** v2 §4, backlog #25 (duplicate confirmed).

### 13. Weak Capability Statements

**Flags:** is designed to, aims to, allows users to, enables teams to (hiding the agent behind capability language).

**Fix:** Say what it does, with a named actor.

**Before:** "The dashboard is designed to provide actionable insights."

**After:** "The dashboard shows which pages lose users."

**Source:** backlog #5.

### 14. Expletive Openings

**Flags:** there is/are, it is [adjective] that, the fact that (wasting words before the subject).

**Fix:** Lead with the subject.

**Before:** "There are several factors that contribute to the delay."

**After:** "Several factors caused the delay."

**Before:** "It is clear that the policy failed."

**After:** "The policy failed."

**Source:** backlog #3.

### 15. Filler, Throat-Clearing, and False Honesty

**Flags:** in order to, due to the fact that, at this point in time, it is important to note that, it's worth mentioning, one might argue, arguably, potentially, could possibly, needless to say, here's the thing, let that sink in, think about it, it goes without saying, in my humble opinion, as you might expect, to be perfectly honest, honestly, to be frank, frankly.

**Fix:** Calibrated confidence (D5). Keep a TRUE hedge only with its reason attached, and match certainty to evidence. Cut the rest. "I think" is allowed only against a named opposing view. Never open with honesty disclaimers; they imply the rest was dishonest.

**Before:** "It is important to note that, in order to achieve this goal, we must leverage cross-functional collaboration."

**After:** "To hit the goal, we need to work together."

**Before:** "To be perfectly honest, the deadline was missed because two vendors shipped late."

**After:** "The deadline was missed because two vendors shipped late."

**Source:** v2 §10, backlog filler adds (duplicates confirmed); Fix reworked per D5 (mechanics: see human-sentences.md).

### 16. Adverbial Safety Nets

**Flags:** ultimately, indeed, essentially, fundamentally, clearly, naturally, after all (as sentence starters or paragraph caps).

**Fix:** Cut. If the adverb carries the whole point, promote the point to a verb.

**Before:** "Ultimately, the success of your startup depends on finding product-market fit."

**After:** "Your startup will fail if nobody wants what you're building."

**Source:** v2 §10, backlog #22 (duplicate confirmed).

### 17. No-Surprise and Safe-to-Say Hedges

**Flags:** it's no surprise that, it's safe to say, in today's fast-paced world (prefacing platitudes with obviousness markers).

**Fix:** Cut the marker. Either state the fact or show a stat and cut the sentence.

**Before:** "In today's fast-paced world, it's no surprise that stress levels are on the rise."

**After:** "Stress levels are rising."

**Source:** v2 §10, backlog #24 (duplicate confirmed).

### 18. Em Dash Overuse (Zero Tolerance)

**Flags:** any em dash or en dash in the final rewrite.

**Fix:** Target zero. Dashes are the single strongest AI punctuation tell. Replace with periods, commas, parentheses, or colons (repertoire: see human-sentences.md).

**Before:** "The term is used by Dutch institutions -- not by the people themselves -- yet the practice continues."

**After:** "The term is used by Dutch institutions, not by the people themselves. The practice continues anyway."

**Source:** v2 §11, D2.

### 19. Chatbot Artifacts and Sycophancy

**Flags:** Great question!, I hope this helps!, Let me know if you'd like me to expand, You're absolutely right, That's an excellent point, Certainly!, Of course!, Here's an overview, Want me to..., Should I continue?, Would you like examples?, I'm glad you asked, I'd be happy to, Absolutely, you're spot on.

**Fix:** Delete. Start with the actual content.

**Before:** "Great question! Here's an overview of the French Revolution. I hope this helps!"

**After:** "The French Revolution began in 1789 when financial crisis and food shortages led to unrest."

**Before:** "Want me to walk you through the setup?"

**After:** "Here is how to set it up."

**Source:** v2 §12, backlog chatbot and sycophancy adds (duplicates confirmed, already in v2).

### 20. Knowledge-Cutoff Disclaimers

**Flags:** as of my last update, based on available information, while specific details are limited, not publicly available, maintains a low profile, likely grew up, it is believed that, not extensively documented in readily available sources, maintains a relatively private personal life.

**Fix:** Say what is known, cite it, or cut the sentence. Do not dress up a guess as fact.

**Before:** "While details about her early life are not publicly available, she likely grew up in a middle-class household."

**After:** "Her early life is not documented in available sources."

**Before:** "As of my last training update in 2024, the company appears to have been established sometime in the 1990s."

**After:** "The company was founded in 1994, according to its registration documents." (Or: "The company's founding date is not documented.")

**Source:** v2 §14, backlog cutoff adds (duplicates confirmed).

### 21. Aphorism Formulas and Pseudo-Profundity

**Flags:** X is the Y of Z, X becomes a trap, X is not a tool but a mirror, the language of, the currency of, the architecture of; every sentence landing like a quotable closer; at its core, at its heart, stripped down to its essentials, [X] is ultimately about, more than just [Y], it's about, not just [literal] but [grandiose].

**Fix:** Replace with the concrete claim underneath the metaphor or philosophy.

**Before:** "Symmetry is the language of trust. Efficiency becomes a trap when teams forget the human layer."

**After:** "Symmetric layouts often feel more predictable. Teams can over-optimize workflows and miss how people actually use them."

**Before:** "At its core, project management is not about spreadsheets; it's about fostering human connection and alignment."

**After:** "Project management is about making sure people ship their work on time."

**Before:** "This is not just a keyboard; it's an extension of your creative mind."

**After:** "This keyboard has mechanical switches and a split layout to reduce wrist strain."

**Source:** v2 §16, backlog #18 and #27 (duplicates confirmed).

### 22. Permission-Granting Endings

**Flags:** And that's okay. Which is fine. That's completely understandable. You don't have to get it right immediately (softening a claim with emotional permission).

**Fix:** Delete the permission. The fact stands on its own.

**Before:** "Most teams miss deadlines. And that's okay."

**After:** "Most teams miss deadlines."

**Source:** backlog #1.

### 23. Future-Perfect Promises

**Flags:** is poised to revolutionize, is set to transform, will continue to drive innovation, is expected to disrupt.

**Fix:** Say what it does now, or admit uncertainty.

**Before:** "The platform is poised to revolutionize how teams collaborate."

**After:** "The platform lets teams share files in real time."

**Source:** backlog #4.

### 24. Pseudo-Scenarios

**Flags:** picture this, imagine a world where, consider the following scenario.

**Fix:** State the example directly.

**Before:** "Imagine a world where your code deploys itself."

**After:** "The tool deploys code automatically."

**Source:** backlog #8.
