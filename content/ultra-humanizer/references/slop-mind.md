# Slop Mind: Psychological AI Habits

> Volatile catalog of model psychology tells. New model generations add entries here; detection patterns may shift with each release.

These are not single phrases but structural tendencies. Watch for them in the overall shape of the text. For sentence level fixes (hedges, rhythm, specificity), see human-sentences.md. For significance inflation and forced closure shapes, see slop-shapes.md.

### 1. Interpretation Instead of Observation

**Flags:** explains motive or meaning where a human would report what happened; "reflected", "revealed", "demonstrated" applied to behavior.

**Fix:** Replace the interpretation with the observable action. Let the reader infer.

Before: "His hesitation reflected deeper uncertainty about the project's direction."

After: "He paused for a few seconds before answering."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #31

### 2. Universalizing

**Flags:** one example becomes a statement about society; "broader shifts", "modern consumers", "reflects how we".

**Fix:** Stay at the scale of what was actually observed.

Before: "This trend reflects broader shifts in how modern consumers engage with technology."

After: "People used the app more after they added notifications."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #33

### 3. Excessive Coherence

**Flags:** paragraphs connect suspiciously smoothly; every transition lands; nothing jumps tracks. Real writing veers, restarts, leaves a rough edge.

**Fix:** Let one paragraph veer into a related tangent or restart the thought instead of bridging neatly.

Before: "Three paragraphs, each opening with a neat transition (Furthermore / Moreover / Consequently) and building one logical staircase."

After: "Two paragraphs build the case. The third drops the bridge and starts over: 'Actually, the part nobody mentions is the support queue.'"

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #34 (Before/After pair and "jumps tracks" test folded from backlog)

### 4. Generic Emotional Vocabulary

**Flags:** names emotions from a distance; "was frustrated", "felt overwhelmed", "was excited".

**Fix:** Show the action the feeling produced. Cut the label.

Before: "She was frustrated."

After: "She deleted the draft and started over."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #35

### 5. Retrospective Certainty

**Flags:** writes causes as obvious after the fact; "ultimately led to", "proved to be", "was inevitable".

**Fix:** Restore the uncertainty people felt at the time. Attribute the causal claim.

Before: "The decision ultimately led to the company's decline."

After: "People inside the company still argue about whether that decision hurt them."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #37

### 6. Metadata Leakage

**Flags:** the text discusses its own significance; "this highlights", "this underscores", "it is worth noting".

**Fix:** Say the thing directly. Drop the frame that announces its importance.

Before: "This highlights the need for better communication."

After: "We need to talk more."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #38

### 7. Calibrated Confidence (reworks "Uniform Confidence")

**Flags:** every statement carries the same flat certainty; no distinction between witnessed fact, inference, and guess. Opposite failure: performative hedges ("I think", "maybe") sprayed evenly to sound humble without tracking evidence.

**Fix:** Match certainty to evidence. Keep a TRUE hedge (one you actually hold) and attach its reason in the same sentence. State witnessed facts flatly. Mark inferences with the reason ("I think X mattered because Y"). Never use "I think" against a named opposing view as a politeness softener; either endorse the uncertainty or take the position. Kill "honestly" and "to be frank" everywhere (see human-sentences.md for the mechanics and the banned list).

Before: "The redesign increased signups. Users preferred the new flow. The old flow was confusing."

After: "Signups rose 20% after the redesign (I know this from the dashboard). I think the shorter form mattered most, because support tickets about signup dropped by half. Whether the old flow was confusing or just slow, I cannot tell from this data."

**Source:** v2 §17 reworked per D5; backlog #39 baseline replaced (flat declarative Before kept as diagnosis, "mix hedges" recipe replaced with reason attached rule)

### 8. Synthetic Fairness

**Flags:** compulsively gives equal airtime; "valid arguments on both sides", "critics and supporters alike".

**Fix:** Report the actual distribution of views in the room, even if lopsided.

Before: "There are valid arguments on both sides."

After: "Most people in the room thought it was a bad idea."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #40

### 9. Fake Personalization

**Flags:** invented credentials to build false rapport; "As a [role] myself", "As someone who has worked in [field]".

**Fix:** Just make the point. The fake credential adds nothing. If real experience informs the claim, state the specific fact, not the role ("I ran support for two years" beats "As someone who has led teams").

Before: "As a developer myself, I know debugging is frustrating."

After: "Debugging is frustrating."

Before: "As a writer myself, I understand the challenges of drafting."

After: "Drafting stalls when you edit mid sentence."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #7 (extra role variants and specific fact rule folded from backlog)

### 10. Fake Precision

**Flags:** specific looking numbers that imply false accuracy; unrounded decimals, suspiciously exact ranges.

**Fix:** Round to human precision or delete the number if it is invented. "About 4 mm" beats "3.81 mm"; "about a dozen" beats "12 to 16 place settings".

Before: "The market will reach $140.55 billion by 2029, growing at 19.2% CAGR."

After: "The market could reach roughly $140 billion by 2029."

**Source:** v2 §17; backlog #2 (round or delete rule and everyday precision examples folded from backlog)

### 11. Fake Specificity

**Flags:** looks concrete, says nothing; "organizations across industries", "changing market dynamics", "adapting to evolving needs". Nouns without referents.

**Fix:** Name the actors and the action. Who, how many, when.

Before: "Organizations across industries are adapting to changing market dynamics."

After: "Three companies we talked to changed their pricing last quarter."

**Source:** backlog #36 (absent from §17, §2, and §9 baselines; folded whole)

### 12. Meaning Driven Rhythm (D6 pointer, no recipe)

**Flags:** uniform sentence length and cadence across paragraphs; every point lands with the same weight; no acceleration or pause where the content demands it.

**Fix:** There is no numeric recipe (no target lengths, no sentence counts). Slow the rhythm at the hard part: longer, heavier sentences where the reader must think. Cut short at the punchline. Fragments only at peaks, never as decoration. Full mechanics live in human-sentences.md; apply them here as diagnosis only.

Before: "Five medium sentences in a row, each making a medium sized point with medium emphasis."

After: "Same content rewritten so the difficult claim gets room (a longer sentence that holds the complication) and the conclusion lands in five words."

**Source:** D6 decision (replaces any fixed rhythm recipe; mechanics: see human-sentences.md)
