# Reflection Journal — Structured Thinking Over Time

## What This Is

A thinking instrument for self-knowledge that compounds. Most skills in the library work within a single session — you think, you decide, you move on. This skill works *across* sessions. It captures your thinking over time, finds patterns you can't see in the moment, and builds a longitudinal record of how your mind works.

You send a dump. It stores, tags, organizes. Later, it shows you what you were circling without realizing it.

## When to Use

- "Help me reflect on today/this week"
- "What patterns do you see in my thinking?"
- "Journal entry"
- "What have I learned recently?"
- "I want to capture this thought for later"
- End of day/week/month reflection
- "Why do I keep making this mistake?"
- "What decisions did I make and why?"

## How It Works

### Input: Accept Dumps

The journal accepts raw, unstructured input:
- End-of-day brain dumps
- "Here's what I'm thinking about..."
- Decision records (what I decided + why)
- Reactions to events, conversations, reading
- Frustrations, wins, surprises
- Questions you can't answer yet

No format required. Just send it.

### Processing: Auto-Tag and Organize

When you send a dump, the journal:

1. **Extracts key themes** — what topics/decisions/patterns are present
2. **Tags automatically** — #decision, #learning, #frustration, #insight, #question, #pattern, #win
3. **Links to related entries** — "this connects to what you wrote on [date] about [topic]"
4. **Identifies recurring themes** — "you've mentioned X three times this week"
5. **Flags unresolved questions** — "you asked this on [date] and haven't answered it"

### Output Modes

**Daily Reflection:**
- What happened today?
- What did I learn?
- What surprised me?
- What am I grateful for?
- What's unresolved?

**Weekly Review:**
- Patterns across the week
- Progress on priorities
- Energy levels and what affected them
- Decisions made and their reasoning
- Recurring themes
- Priorities for next week

**Monthly/Quarterly:**
- Bigger picture trends
- Goal tracking — am I on course?
- Course corrections needed
- What patterns keep repeating?
- What have I avoided looking at?
- What's changed in how I think?

**Decision Journal:**
- What I decided
- Why I decided it
- What I expected to happen
- What actually happened (filled in later)
- What I'd do differently
- Calibration: was my confidence appropriate?

### Pattern Recognition Across Entries

The real value emerges over time. The journal looks for:

- **Recurring themes** — topics that keep appearing but never resolve
- **Contradictions** — positions that conflict across different entries
- **Evolution** — how your thinking has shifted on a topic
- **Avoidance** — questions you keep asking but never answering
- **Energy patterns** — what gives energy vs drains it
- **Decision quality** — comparing predictions to outcomes
- **Blind spots** — topics you never write about (absence is also data)

## Output Format

### For a dump entry:
```
## Journal Entry: [Date]

### Raw Input
[What you sent]

### Extracted Themes
- [Theme 1]
- [Theme 2]

### Tags
#tag1 #tag2 #tag3

### Connections
- Related to: [previous entry on date] about [topic]
- Recurring: [theme] appeared [N] times this [week/month]

### Unresolved
- [Question you asked but haven't answered]
```

### For a review:
```
## [Daily/Weekly/Monthly] Review: [Date Range]

### Themes This Period
1. [Most prominent theme] — [N] mentions
2. [Second theme] — [N] mentions

### Decisions Made
- [Decision] on [date] — reasoning: [summary]

### Patterns Noticed
- [Pattern]: [description]

### Unresolved Questions
- [Question] — first asked [date], still open

### Energy
- High energy: [what gave energy]
- Low energy: [what drained energy]

### For Next Period
- Priority: [what to focus on]
- Watch for: [pattern to monitor]
- Question to answer: [unresolved to tackle]
```

## Storage

Journal entries accumulate in a structured folder:
```
journal/
├── entries/
│   ├── 2026-03-17-dump-01.md
│   ├── 2026-03-17-dump-02.md
│   └── ...
├── reviews/
│   ├── weekly-2026-03-17.md
│   └── monthly-2026-03.md
├── decisions/
│   └── decision-log.md
└── patterns/
    └── recurring-themes.md
```

- `gap-awareness` — "what have I been avoiding writing about?"
- `mental-models` — frameworks for interpreting patterns
- `premortem-retrospective` (in thinking-opponent) — structured review of specific events

## Anti-Patterns

- Don't force daily entries — write when there's something to capture
- Don't perform for the journal — it's for you, not an audience
- Don't skip the "why" in decision journaling — "what I decided" without "why" is useless later
- Don't ignore recurring themes — if something keeps appearing, it's trying to tell you something
- Don't treat pattern recognition as one-time — it compounds, check periodically

## The Core Insight

You can't see your own patterns in real-time. You need distance — temporal distance — to notice what you keep doing, thinking, avoiding. The journal creates that distance. It's not about productivity or self-improvement theater. It's about knowing yourself better because you have a record of yourself to examine.
